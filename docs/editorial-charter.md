# Editorial Charter

**Status:** project-wide editorial baseline
**Applies to:** recipe selection, writing, art direction, image generation, typography, layout, and future collection features

## Core Position

This project is a growing digital recipe collection about contemporary regional plant-based cooking. Its recipe pages can carry the expressive density of a small design publication, but the website as a whole is not framed as a fixed magazine issue. It is not a nostalgic cookbook and does not attempt to recreate traditional German domestic culture.

Regional cooking is treated as a starting point rather than a boundary: ingredients that grow in, or are readily available across, Central Europe are combined with international flavours, techniques, formats, and ideas. The result should feel current, urban, open, and practical.

## Editorial Values

The cooking is:

- plant-based
- urban and contemporary
- open to international influence
- experimental without becoming impractical
- seasonal where the recipe genuinely supports it
- attentive to texture, preparation state, and material qualities
- suitable for everyday cooking
- presented with the care of a designed publication

The recipes may be familiar, newly combined, or regionally named. Their treatment must remain contemporary. Regional identity should be legible through ingredients, season, context, and naming—not through decorative folklore.

## Explicit Exclusions

The project must not use the following as shorthand for regionality:

- romanticised homeland imagery
- half-timbered houses or picturesque village scenery
- folk costumes or folkloric decoration
- retro country-house styling
- staged old bakery interiors
- sentimental “grandmother's kitchen” framing
- rustic nostalgia as the main emotional device
- historical props that do not serve the recipe

These exclusions apply to generated images, graphic decoration, public copy, and naming. Warmth is welcome; nostalgia is not the mechanism used to create it.

## Cultural and Visual Reference Field

The collection takes more inspiration from contemporary cafés, modern patisserie, design magazines, architecture, editorial design, independent food zines, and carefully art-directed cookbooks than from traditional recipe books.

The intended feeling is warm but not old-fashioned, designed but not sterile, playful but not childish, and handmade without appearing improvised.

## Visual Language

The reference poster remains the primary visual authority. The website translates its qualities into responsive layers rather than reproducing it as one fixed image.

The shared language combines:

- one continuous tactile paper surface
- collage and mixed-media composition
- restrained watercolour fields and pigment variation
- hand-drawn rules, arrows, small doodles, and annotations
- believable, appetising food photography or photographic illustration
- open contemporary editorial layouts
- asymmetry, visual rhythm, and intentional empty space

Ingredients, food textures, colour, and craft processes are the protagonists. Decorative elements connect the composition but must not compete with the recipe.

## Food and Material Direction

“Material-oriented” is primarily an internal art-direction principle, not necessarily public-facing marketing language. It means paying close attention to crumb, creaminess, crispness, translucency, toasted surfaces, dough tension, frying colour, folds, glaze, grain, ceramic, glass, paper fibre, and pigment.

The food should remain the most realistic and materially convincing element on every page. Layout texture must never compensate for inaccurate or unappetising food.

## Editorial Voice

Public writing should be precise, warm, and confident. Avoid heritage clichés, exaggerated claims, wellness promises, and theory-heavy design language. Explain the regional starting point in plain language and allow international influences to appear naturally.

Use “contemporary regional cooking” as an editorial description when useful, but do not repeatedly label every recipe as new, modern, or innovative. The treatment should demonstrate that position.

## Recipe Selection Test

A recipe belongs in the collection when it can answer most of these questions clearly:

1. Which ingredients or preparation context connect it to Central Europe?
2. What technique, flavour, or formal idea moves it beyond a nostalgic regional reading?
3. What material quality makes the dish visually and sensorially distinctive?
4. Can it be prepared realistically in everyday life?
5. Can its story be communicated through one strong hero and a concise process sequence?

## Design Decision Test

Before approving a page or asset, ask:

1. Does this feel like a contemporary food-zine page rather than a conventional recipe card?
2. Is warmth coming from food, paper, colour, and craft rather than nostalgia?
3. Are the ingredients and process more prominent than decoration?
4. Is the result editorial and expressive without becoming visually chaotic?
5. Would the same decision still make sense when the collection contains twenty recipes?

## Website Architecture

The landing page is a functional, continuously growing recipe index. It should preserve the shared paper, collage, watercolour, and hand-drawn language while giving direct access to recipes and scalable discovery controls. It must not imply a closed issue, edition, or volume.

Recipe detail pages carry more visual density and may read like individual contemporary food-zine spreads. The distinction is intentional: the index organises the collection, while each recipe provides the immersive editorial experience.

## Collection Principle

Each recipe may have its own visual character, but all recipes belong to one visual and editorial system. Shared paper, typography, prompt structure, asset families, responsive rules, and editorial standards provide continuity. Dish-specific palette shifts, illustrations, notes, and compositions provide variation.
